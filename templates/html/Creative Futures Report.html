Our second annual Anonymous Creative Futures survey arrives at a critical moment for the creative ecosystem. Economic precarity is up. So are market demands for more work and content. People are drowning in digital overload, but starved for community and real connection. The perspectives and ideas shared in this document are a pulse of where we are at this moment of time. More than 300 artists and creative people, across mediums and practices, weighed in. In your words and opinions, this is where we are now.

AI, AUTOMATION & THE QUESTION OF HUMANNESS

Fears of displacement, resistance through imperfection, and reluctant adoption

OPEN

AI & AUTOMATION

ON BEING DISPLACED

"we are being automated out of our own creativity"

"AI tools have dampened the human spirit of creativity and curiosity. I'm worried about what an uncreative, uncurious populace will look like"

"I worry that I am being robbed of agency and interest in generating personal works, especially when it can be easily robbed of substance by a simple claim that I didn't make it. The author is dying on the writing table."

"I'm worried that manual exploratory work will soon be seen as outdated, unnecessary, and costly (which is the enjoyable part of a design process for me)"

"I'm afraid we're headed for a future where practicing a skill and training a muscle to get good and then excellent at something will become a kind of pointless goal."

"I'm worried that AI has wiped out the entry-level jobs that used to be the stepping stones for learning new skills and breaking into an industry."

ON RESISTANCE AND DIFFERENTIATION

"Creatives will lean into imperfect absurdism. People's art will become more chaotic, less pretty as a way to differentiate themselves from AI"

"More imperfect digital work, work that has to show how it is human."

"handmade as anti-ai 'proof of effort' will continue to be explored. Effort and time spent will grow as a metric of value"

"I'll need to spend more time sharing my process of making art, rather than just the end result, which will be increasingly devalued and commoditised to the point of being meaningless."

"Evident human made flaws in work will become more valuable as erstaz A.I. perfectionism pervades everything."

"I can do it without AI, and get the joy of doing it which AI doesn't provide its users - what joy it is to feel this ability… it's like summiting Everest without oxygen support."

ON AI SLOP AND THE FLOOD

"The tidal wave of garbage coming from companies trying to replace creatives with AI tooling is already staggering and it's going to get worse."

"that people will get used to mediocre AI art and forget what real creativity feels like"

"multimedia slop warfare"

"More intention beneath the surface, more slop on the surface"

"Metaslop - Creatives using slop in the same way that technical issues like audio feedback and media buffering were fodder for new work"

"There is going to be a lot more content. We either get sick of it or all just consume more AI native content."

ON UTILIZING AI ANYWAY

"utilizing AI but also resisting it"

"AI is only a tool, but in hindsight its presence is alerting the world that change is necessary"

"Those who reject it completely may be left behind or doomed to flounder in a bog of drudgery. Those who rely on it to replace thinking and creativity will find those muscles atrophied when they need them most."

"I've been having fun using Claude (AI) as a sounding board for my ideas. I'm not interested in having AI create for me, but I find it incredibly useful for organizing thoughts and playing with structure."

ECONOMIC PRECARITY & THE BUSINESS OF CREATIVITY

The math does not add up, but we are doing it anyway

OPEN

ECONOMIC REALITY

ON FINANCIAL INSECURITY

"I've had three years where I've worked full-time, but rarely actually got paid. We're moving some place cheaper to help our budget. I don't know how long I can keep this up."

"The perceivable equations for maintaining a life increasingly do not add up"

"I worry that this world wasn't made for me and people like me. I worry that I will die alone."

"Everything feels like a scramble."

"Harder to feed my family = less art more boring work"

"income, debts, less time to create, feel like I missed my chance. Over 40 and being a mom feels like I will never 'emerge'. It is too late now."

"The overwhelming administrative burden of needing to constantly seek out new opportunities for funding my practice drains an ever-increasing share of my time to actually practice as an artist."

ON THE CHANGING MARKET

"It's going to be a financial bloodbath that sparks a creative revolution."

"Think art will become a total wealthy class game."

"People who have creative jobs as their primary source of income may struggle, but creation as creation will not"

"I think the economy is going to collapse in disastrous way sometime before Q2 of 2026, because of the AI bubble, and then almost everything will get worse."

"The significant increases in opportunities and salaries that occurred during COVID have been eliminated and then some."

ON THE IMPORTANCE OF BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING

"the importance of understanding business as a creative person"

"the leftist who can embrace financial practicalities and professionalism while trying to live their values and make art they are proud of are the ones who will protect their focus enough to produce at scale"

"Artist Corporations and the development of them as a powerful tool that creatives can use to claim their power."

"Now that I accepted I will never make a living from art, I am looking forward to meeting interesting and clever people and having fun."
