THE PROBLEM

A great team can still build the *wrong system. Requirements live — restated and contradictory — across Word, Excel, PDFs, Jira, Confluence, meeting notes, and chat threads. Each artefact is useful for communication; together, they are a *poor computational structure. The business analyst becomes the *human *integration *layer.

01 — THE TECHNICAL GAP

Artefacts without relationships.

Most tools store documents and generate isolated summaries — not the knowledge relationships between artefacts.

02 — THE PROCESS GAP

Intent drifts while requirements evolve.

Validation happens at fixed review points while requirements keep changing in conversation, design, and delivery.

03 — THE STRATEGIC GAP

Failure compounds downstream.

Requirements failure produces costly rework, missed deadlines, and lost trust — rarely quantified early enough to prevent it.

04 — METHODOLOGY

Design Science Research.

The contribution is an artefact: problem identification, framework design, prototype, evaluation, and deployment guidance.

05 — THE FRAMEWORK

One knowledge, two forms.

An LLM-Wiki gives stakeholders a readable, versioned space; a knowledge graph makes requirements machine-queryable and traceable.

06 — THE REASONING LAYER

Six agents. One auditable pipeline.

Ingestion, architecture, governance, story writing, validation, and integration hand off through a single traceable flow — every step logged, from raw input to publish-ready requirement.

THE REASONING LAYER
