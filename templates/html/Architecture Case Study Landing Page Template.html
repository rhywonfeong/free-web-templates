The challenge was to site a substantial family home within a dense ponderosa pine forest without disrupting the existing root systems or the visual rhythm of the trees.

The site presented a unique topography: a steep granite ridge falling away into a ravine. Rather than flattening the land, we chose to elevate the primary living volume on steel piers, allowing the terrain to flow uninterrupted beneath the structure. This gesture not only preserved the watershed but created a sense of living within the canopy itself.

Materiality was drawn directly from the context. The exterior is clad in Shou Sugi Ban cedar, mirroring the dark bark of the surrounding pines and providing natural fire resistance. Inside, the palette softens to warm white oak and expansive glazing, framing specific views of the granite outcroppings like living artwork.
