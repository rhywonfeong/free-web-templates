Mission Overview

As humanity prepares to live and work on the Moon, one question matters deeply: what is happening beneath the surface?

MDMS deploys a network of portable seismic sensors via the Lunar Terrain Vehicle across the lunar South Pole — monitoring moonquakes and meteorite impacts in real time to guide safe habitats, infrastructure, and long-term exploration.

Objective 01

Understand Seismic Activity

Monitor natural moonquakes and impacts to reveal the Moon's internal dynamics.

Objective 02

Map Subsurface Structure

Analyze wave propagation to chart the lunar crust, mantle, and core composition.

Objective 03

Define Safe Zones

Assess seismic risk to identify stable, low-risk regions for human exploration.

01 — The Instrument

A seismic ear, planted in lunar regolith.

Each MDMS unit combines a high-sensitivity seismic sensor, autonomous power, communications, and an integrated auger drill that anchors the instrument directly into the lunar surface.

03 — Lunar Seismology

The Moon is not silent.

Deep moonquakes pulse hundreds of kilometers below the surface. Shallow quakes crack the crust. Meteorite impacts ring the Moon like a bell. MDMS listens to all of it — building on Apollo-era seismic data and extending research to the unexplored South Pole.
