Manuscript Wealth

Historical reliability is measured by the number of copies and the time gap between the event and the writing. The New Testament has no rival in ancient literature.

Plato ~7 copies
Homer's Iliad ~643 copies
New Testament 24,000+ copies
Artifact
Papyrus P52

Discovered in Egypt, this fragment of John's Gospel dates to ~125 AD. This puts the physical evidence within one generation of the original writings—a statistical anomaly in ancient history.

Stones Don't Lie

The Bible is grounded in real history, locations, and people—verified by secular excavations.

House of David

Critics claimed King David was a myth until the Tel Dan Stele (1993) was found containing the inscription "House of David" by an enemy king.

Pontius Pilate

The Pilate Stone discovered in Caesarea Maritima confirmed his existence and exact Roman title ("Prefect"), validating the Gospel accounts.

Erastus of Corinth

Paul sends greetings from Erastus, the city treasurer (Rom 16:23). A pavement stone was found in Corinth: "Erastus... laid this pavement."

CRITERION OF EMBARRASSMENT

Too Counter-Productive to be Fake

Invented propaganda makes the heroes look perfect. The Bible does the opposite. It includes details that would have been embarrassing and hurtful to the cause in the 1st Century—unless they were simply true.

Women Witnesses

In 1st Century Judea, a woman's testimony was inadmissible in court. If you were faking the Resurrection, you would never say women found the tomb first.

Cowardly Leaders

The authors record their own leader (Peter) denying Jesus three times and the disciples hiding in fear. Hero myths don't do this.
