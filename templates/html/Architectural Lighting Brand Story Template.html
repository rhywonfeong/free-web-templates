SALT — Our Story

A Quiet Beginning

What began as a quiet pursuit among architects is now open to everyone. We started with a simple observation:

“Lighting was everywhere but rarely understood.”

Too bright. Too harsh. Too thoughtless.

We believed light could be more.

A Different way to think about light

Light is not decoration.

It defines space. Reveals material. Shapes how people feel.

At SALT, we design light not as an object, but as a behavior within architecture.

The Pursuit of Intent

From the beginning, we did not chase scale. We focused on fundamentals:

  • Consistency
  • Comfort
  • Control

Because great lighting is not noticed. It is experienced.

Engineering the Invisible

Every SALT luminaire is built with purpose. From chipset to optics, from thermal design to finish each element works as part of a system.

Not to stand out. But to disappear into architecture. And let the space speak.

Learning from light itself

Our process begins with observation.

How daylight moves. How shadows soften edges. How light defines depth without excess.

These are not effects. They are principles.

We translate them into products that feel natural, effortless, and precise.

The Evolution of Lumière

In the early years, SALT existed within design projects. Working alongside architects, we shaped spaces through light quietly, carefully, intentionally. Over time, something became clear: This way of thinking about light should not remain limited.

A period of learning. Breaking things down.

2012

The Beginning

The idea of SALT began as a question. Why does some light feel right, while most do not?

2013

Understanding the anatomy

We moved into the fundamentals.

Lenses LED behavior Heat sinks Drivers

Every component studied, not individually but as part of a system.

2014

Precision over approximation

What began as curiosity became discipline. We tested, refined, and questioned. Not how to make light brighter but how to make it better.

2017

The Brand Emerges

Intentionality should not be a luxury. We introduced SALT not to sell more lights, but to make professional architectural lighting accessible to everyday spaces.
