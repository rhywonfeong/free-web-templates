A Statement on Work, Intelligence and Responsibility.

Artificial Intelligence has been in development for decades. What has changed is not its existence, but its impact.

For the first time, intelligent systems can generate, adapt, and improve at a scale that directly affects how people work, learn, and create value. This shift is not theoretical. It is already visible across industries, roles, and societies.

Artificial Intelligence increases productivity and reduces the time required to perform many tasks. As a result, work will change — not gradually, but structurally. Some jobs will disappear. Others will be reshaped. New forms of work will emerge.

This is neither good nor bad by default.It is a transition.

The future of work cannot be fully planned, but it can be approached with clarity. Technology alone does not determine outcomes. Human decisions — economic, organizational, and societal — do.

We do not believe fear is a useful response.We also do not believe denial is responsible.

Artificial Intelligence cannot be undone or ignored. Attempts to slow it through avoidance or prohibition only increase the risk of unpreparedness. The question is not whether AI will change work, but how people will be supported through that change.

We believe access matters.We believe understanding matters.We believe people should not be left alone in this transition.

This moment requires new ways of working, learning, and collaborating — grounded in reality, not hype; in responsibility, not nostalgia.

The future of work is not a distant concept.It is already unfolding.

Our role is not to predict it.Our role is to participate in shaping it.
