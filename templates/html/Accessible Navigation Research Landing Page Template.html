The problem

Independent campus travel is still unsolved at scale.

Existing systems can work well, but they depend on infrastructure, specialist scanning, or a live human operator. That makes deployment slow, expensive, and difficult to scale across entire campuses.

Beacon-based systems

Require Bluetooth beacons installed and maintained at scale across buildings and doorways.

Scanned indoor maps

Depend on specialized capture teams and repeated data collection to stay current.

Remote assistance

Works in the moment, but every trip needs another person on the line.

How it works

Two apps, one route model, one walk to make a path reusable.

EchoEcho turns a specialist-guided route into a sequence of waypoints with headings, hazards, and annotations. Students then follow that route using haptic patterns and spoken prompts.

1. Record

An O&M specialist or trained volunteer walks a campus route in the admin app, capturing GPS coordinates, headings, hazards, photos, and voice notes at each decision point.

2. Request

A student opens the app and speaks a destination. The system matches the destination to a building and selects a published route with the right ordered waypoints.

3. Navigate

The phone guides with directional vibration patterns and spoken annotations, using GPS outdoors and pedestrian dead reckoning to maintain continuity between buildings.

The iOS speech + haptics constraint
A real platform limitation, not a product quirk

When speech recognition is active, the Taptic Engine is silenced. EchoEcho solves this with a mutex that queues haptic cues while voice input is open, then plays them immediately after the microphone closes.

Haptic research

The core interface is vibration, not audio.

Audio competes with traffic, conversation, and environmental listening. Haptics occupy a separate sensory channel, which makes them central to safe, practical navigation.

Current evidence

A 2022 PMC study reported 82–90% recognition accuracy for rhythm patterns in seated conditions and about 70% under cognitive load. EchoEcho is designed to test these encodings under real walking conditions with visually impaired participants.

Rhythm-based
Default

Distinct rhythmic character for each direction: felt rather than counted.

Pulse counting

One buzz, two buzzes, three buzzes. Easy to teach, but counting may break down under load.

Duration encoding

Short, medium, and long pulses reduce counting, but the middle duration can blur during movement.

Proximity ramp

Buzz frequency increases as the waypoint approaches, layering spatial awareness on top of direction.

Architecture

A shared route engine across admin, student, and backend systems.

The system is built around ordered waypoint sequences, shared geo utilities, haptic timing definitions, and role-scoped data access for route creation and navigation.

  • Route recording and review lifecycle
  • Campus management and hazard marking
  • On-device haptic lab for timing arrays
