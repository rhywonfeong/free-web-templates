The Damning Record

These are not allegations — they are documented facts, sourced from court records, congressional testimony, and his own public statements.

1973 · Housing Discrimination

DOJ Filed Suit for Refusing to Rent to Black Tenants

The Department of Justice sued Donald Trump and his father Fred for systematically refusing to rent apartments to Black applicants. Trump Properties coded applications from Black renters with a "C" for "Colored." The Trumps settled — without admitting guilt — but later violated the agreement. Source: DOJ v. Trump, 1973.

1989 · Targeting Innocent Black Men

$500,000 Ad Demanding Death Penalty for the Central Park Five

Trump spent half a million dollars on full-page newspaper ads demanding the execution of five Black and Latino teenagers for a crime they did not commit. After DNA evidence exonerated them — he refused to apologize and continued calling them guilty into 2019. Source: NY Times, 1989; 2019 interview.

2017 · Charlottesville

"Very Fine People on Both Sides" — Defending Neo-Nazis

After white nationalists marched chanting "Jews will not replace us" and a neo-Nazi murdered Heather Heyer, Trump said there were "very fine people on both sides." His own chief of staff John Kelly called it a "defining moment of dishonor." Source: White House press conference, Aug 15, 2017.

2018 · "Shithole Countries"

Rejected Haitian & African Immigrants — "Why Can't We Get People from Norway?"

In an Oval Office meeting, Trump referred to Haiti and African nations as "shithole countries" and said he wanted immigrants from Norway instead. Two Republican senators confirmed the remarks under oath. The UN called it "shocking and shameful." Source: Senators Durbin & Graham testimony; WaPo Jan 12, 2018.

2018–2021 · Family Separation

5,500+ Brown Children Deliberately Torn From Their Parents

Trump's "Zero Tolerance" policy deliberately separated over 5,500 brown and Black asylum-seeking children from their families as a deliberate deterrent. Hundreds remain separated. Medical groups documented lasting trauma. The ACLU called it "government-sponsored child abuse." Source: ACLU, HHS Inspector General, 2021.

2017 · Muslim Ban

Travel Ban Targeting 7 Muslim-Majority Nations — Courts Called It Religious Discrimination

Trump's executive order banned travel from seven Muslim-majority nations days after promising a "total and complete shutdown of Muslims entering the United States." Federal courts initially blocked it as unconstitutional religious discrimination before a modified version was upheld. Source: EO 13769; 9th & 4th Circuit rulings, 2017.
