Does any of this sound familiar?

One day you can't miss. The next, you're back to the same putting woes you've had for months.

Some days your speed is off. Other days you couldn't read a green if your life depended on it.

You've tried the tips. You've adjusted your grip, your stance, your pre-shot routine. Maybe a mate gave you advice. Maybe even a professional did. And for a while, it felt like it was working.

Then it wasn't.

Here's the truth nobody tells you.

It's not your stroke. It's not your read. It's not your attitude.

Watch a child play golf. No hesitation. No second-guessing. Just a look at the hole, a breath, and a stroke. That fearlessness isn't talent — it's the absence of overthinking.

The best putters in the world aren't technically perfect. They're intuitively confident. And that intuition isn't something you're born with. It's built — through repetition, volume, and most importantly, pressure .

When there are no stakes in your practice, your brain doesn't adapt. You can roll fifty balls at a hole every evening and make zero real progress, because nothing is on the line. There's no consequence for missing. No feeling of a win or a loss.

Your brain needs a reason to care — and right now, your putting practice isn't giving it one.

This is exactly why SuperPutt exists.

SuperPutt combines competition, the feel of a hole-by-hole round of golf, and real consequences for not executing — all inside a 15-minute putting game you can play on any green, alone or with a mate.

No equipment to buy. No setup time. Just a putter, a scorecard, and suddenly — practice has stakes.

When you play SuperPutt, every putt means something. You're not just rolling balls. You're competing. You're tracking. You're building the pressure tolerance that separates a nervous putter from a fearless one.

That's the game. That's the point. And it works.

The putting green will start to feel different.

After a few weeks of playing SuperPutt regularly, something shifts. You'll start approaching putts with a quieter mind. The read feels more instinctive. Your pace control becomes natural rather than calculated.

But it doesn't stop on the green.

That same composure — the ability to commit, execute, and move on — bleeds into the rest of your game. Off the tee. Around the green. In the moments that used to get inside your head.

And if you're playing with a mate? You'll be the one they're watching, wondering what changed.

How to Play SuperPutt

SuperPutt is the full competitive experience whether you're squaring up against a friend or taking on the course by yourself.

Either way — it takes about 45 minutes for 2 players and 30 minutes by yourself. This is almost certainly going to become a weekly ritual for you. Here's everything you need to know to get started.

The Course

SuperPutt is played over 12 holes per game, across 3 games — 36 holes in total.

The course is made up of two types of holes:

🟡

Birdie Holes — Holes 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12. Putts ranging from 3 to 8 metres.

⚪

Eagle Holes — Holes 4 and 10. Putts ranging from 10 to 15 metres.

Scoring

The course has a par of 24 . Every hole is a par 2 .

Here's how every putt is scored:

Result What It Means Score
Eagle Hole it in one putt (Eagle Holes 4 & 10 only) −2
Birdie Hole it in one putt (any Birdie Hole) −1
Par Two putts — but only if your first putt passes the hole 0
Mistake Left short on the first putt — or missed the second after going past +1

⚠️ The most important rule in SuperPutt: A Par is only a Par if your first putt goes past the hole. Leave it short and miss the second — that's a Mistake. This rule exists for a reason. It forces commitment on every single putt. No more tentative, die-at-the-hole strokes. You have to mean it.

How to Keep Score

Every player marks their card with a single X in one of three columns for each hole:

  • M Mistake (+1)
  • P Par (0)
  • B Birdie or Eagle (−1 or −2)

At the bottom of each game you'll see two rows: Total and Diff.

  • Total The raw count of Birdies/Eagles made in that game
  • Diff Birdies/Eagles minus Mistakes = your net score for that game

After three games, add your three Diff scores together. That's your final number. Lowest score wins.

Ways to Play

Whether you're practicing solo or taking a friend's money, SuperPutt adapts to your session.

Solo Play

Play all three games yourself. Track your total diff. If you're shooting below +5 on your first few attempts, you're doing well. Scratch putters should aim for zero or better.

  • Takes roughly 30 minutes
  • Benchmarks your true putting skill

Playing against your playing partner (Match Play)

The added pressure of watching your opponent hole a birdie putt before you step up is where the real growth happens.

If player A conquers the course during any of the three rounds and Player B fails to do so, Player A will win the game.

If neither of the players conquer the course, the winner is determined by subtracting the amount of birdies / eagles they make from the amount of mistakes they have made over the three rounds.

For example: If player A makes 10 mistakes and 5 birdies over the three rounds, then their final score is +5.

If player B were to make 10 mistakes and 4 birdies and 1 eagle over the three rounds, then their final score is +4, thus winning the game.

In the scenario where both players have the same score after three rounds, the player with the most birdies will be the winner.

  • Takes roughly 45 minutes
  • Highest pressure simulation
