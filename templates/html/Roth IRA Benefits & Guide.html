The Basics

What is a Roth IRA?
And why do you need one?

A Roth IRA is a retirement account where your money grows tax‑free. You contribute after‑tax money today, and qualified withdrawals after age 59½ are completely tax‑free.

"Your investments can grow in two ways: companies can pay you a share of their profits (dividends), and the value of your investments can rise (capital gains). Both work together to build wealth while you stay invested."

Tax-Free

Tax-Free Growth & Withdrawal

Because you already paid taxes on the money you put in, all the growth, dividends, and capital gains are yours to keep, tax-free, when you retire.

Flexibility

Withdraw Contributions

"You can withdraw all of your contributions, not your earnings, anytime tax‑free."

This makes it a remarkably flexible tool for young investors who might fear locking away their money forever.

Control

No RMDs & Long-Term Growth

Unlike traditional IRAs, Roth IRAs have no Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) during your lifetime. You control when, or if, you take the money out.

Comparing Accounts

How does a Roth IRA
compare to others?

Understanding the difference between account types is crucial to maximizing your tax advantages.

Roth IRA

  • Contributions are after-tax.
  • Withdrawals in retirement are 100% tax-free.
  • Withdraw contributions anytime tax-free.
  • No Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs).
  • Best if you expect to be in a higher tax bracket later.

Traditional IRA

  • Contributions are often tax-deductible now.
  • Withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income.
  • Penalties for early withdrawal.
  • Subject to Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) at age 73.

401(k) & Savings

401(k)

Offered by employers, often with a match (free money!). Usually taxed like a Traditional IRA upon withdrawal.

Savings Account

Extremely low interest. Growth doesn't beat inflation. Earnings are taxed yearly. Bad for long-term wealth building.

Time is Money

Why starting young matters.

Start Early, Grow More

"The sooner you begin investing, the more years you have to max out your annual Roth IRA limits. Each year you contribute adds another layer of growth — giving your money more time to build on itself."

Long-Term Security

"Starting young gives your investments decades to build stability. Small, consistent contributions now can create a strong financial safety net later."

Exponential Returns

"Your money earns interest, and that interest earns even more interest. Over many years, most of your total balance comes from growth, not what you put in."

The Building Blocks of Wealth

Best Long-Term Investments
for a Roth IRA.

Understanding what to buy is just as important as opening the account. Here is a simple guide to the four main ways to grow your money over decades.

Index Funds

Instead of guessing which one company will win, an index fund buys a tiny piece of every company in a market. It is low-cost, low-stress, and historically very safe over long periods.

ETFs

Exchange-Traded Funds are exactly like index funds, but you can trade them instantly throughout the day like regular stock. They are very flexible and perfect for beginners.

Dividend Stocks

Certain companies are so profitable that they pay you cash just for owning their stock. This is called a dividend. You can automatically reinvest these to buy even more shares.

S&P 500 Funds

A single fund that tracks the 500 biggest U.S. companies (like Apple, Microsoft, Amazon). It is widely considered the ultimate long-term wealth engine for any retirement account.
