Private Villa 2024

Al-Ula
Sanctuary

A monolithic residence sculpted from local sandstone, designed to merge seamlessly with the ancient desert canyons of Saudi Arabia.

Project Data

1,200 sqm

Architecture, Landscape

Material Palette

Riyadh Limestone
Oxidized Bronze
Clay Plaster

The objective was to create a modern interpretation of the traditional courtyard house, providing respite from the harsh desert sun while framing the dramatic rock formations.

Located within a UNESCO World Heritage region, the site demanded extreme sensitivity. Our approach was subtraction rather than addition—carving voids into a solid geometric volume to create shaded microclimates. The central atrium serves as a modern Majlis, a gathering space cooled by passive ventilation and a central water feature reflecting the sky.

The material selection echoes the geology of the Hejaz region. Walls of rammed earth and locally sourced limestone provide thermal mass, keeping the interior cool during the day and releasing warmth at night. Mashrabiya screens, reinterpreted in oxidized bronze, filter the blinding sunlight into gentle, geometric patterns that dance across the floors throughout the day.

Architectural Strategy

The plan is oriented to minimize solar gain while maximizing views of the canyon. The public wing is separated from the private quarters by the central water garden, ensuring privacy for family members during social gatherings.
