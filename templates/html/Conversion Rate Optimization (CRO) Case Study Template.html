How we surpassed the business goal in 10 months

A structured CRO programme combining analytics, behavioural insight, and iterative experimentation — not just a one‑off redesign.

  1. Step 01

    GA4 setup & analysis

    • Implemented key event tracking across the funnel.
    • Analysed high‑traffic pages and their funnel contribution.
    • Broke down traffic and conversions by channel & device.
    • Compared new vs returning user behaviour for intent signals.
  2. Step 02

    Heatmaps & session recordings

    • Mapped out real user journeys across key flows.
    • Used clickmaps and scrollmaps to spot dead‑zones and friction.
  3. Step 03

    Social listening & qual research

    • Analysed Reddit and other forums for common kitchen restoration fears and objections.
    • Turned language directly from customers into positioning, FAQs, and microcopy.
  4. Step 04

    CRO roadmap & design system

    • Prioritised ideas using ICE (Impact, Confidence, Ease).
    • Built a lightweight design system to make future tests consistent and fast to ship.
    • Fixed visual hierarchy and typography site‑wide so key actions are always obvious.
    • Improved product discovery with a reworked navigation and dedicated filters page.
  5. Step 05

    12 A/B tests — 8 winners, 4 learners

    We ran a continuous testing programme instead of betting everything on one large redesign.

    8 winning tests

    1. New hero with clear value proposition + primary CTA.
    2. Added USP strip with 3 key benefits and review badge.
    3. Introduced service timeline on homepage and “How it works”.
    4. Navigation changes to surface high‑intent paths faster.
    5. Layout and copy refinements across quote & contact flows.

    4 learning tests (what didn’t work)

    1. Seasonal offer landing page split test — more distraction, fewer quotes.
    2. “Big bang” homepage redesign with too many changes shipped at once.
    3. Multi‑step quote form — reduced perceived friction but added too many clicks.
    4. One offer‑heavy layout that pulled focus away from core service proof.

Key takeaways

A strong, specific hero value proposition on a service website can move conversions significantly — without adding more traffic.

Continuous CRO (small, validated steps) is more reliable than one large redesign that bundles many changes at once.

“Winning” tests matter — but so do “learning” tests that prevent costly missteps and inform better experiments.
