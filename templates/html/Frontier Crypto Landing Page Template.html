01. Genesis

THE ORIGIN MYTH

(the real one — that's the point)

In 1990, three people who had no business being in the same room started a foundation. A software billionaire , a cypherpunk engineer, and a cattle rancher who wrote lyrics for the Grateful Dead.

What united them was a RAID . The early '90s government had decided the strange new world of modems and bulletin boards was a threat , and started kicking down doors — seizing computers from people who'd broken no law, treating a frontier full of curious kids like a crime scene .

So they named the thing everyone was fighting over. They called it the electronic frontier — a territory as real as any in the Old West, and just as contested. And they built the Electronic Frontier Foundation to make sure the people who lived there had rights .

That's the lore. We didn't write it. We just showed up thirty-odd years later, on a new frontier — an on-chain one — and realized the fight never ended. The giants just changed clothes. Surveillance got cheaper. Speech got easier to delete. The frontier still needs homesteaders willing to pay to keep it free.

02. The Scripture

THE FRONTIER WAS NEVER EMPTY. ■

It was homesteaded by anyone with a modem and a reason. We built towns out of forums, law out of norms, and cathedrals out of open source. And every single generation, the weary giants come to fence what they didn't build — to meter the air, to read the mail, to decide which words are allowed to survive.

We are not asking permission. We are pooling a toll.

We are not a charity. We are a memecoin — loud, volatile, and completely aware of what we are. But the giants have never been beaten by seriousness alone. Sometimes the fence gets torn down by a mob with a good joke and a shared wallet.

Homestead the frontier. Pay the toll. Keep it free.
— The Settlers
