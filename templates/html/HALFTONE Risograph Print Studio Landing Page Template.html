The Process

From pocket
to paper.

Four steps, one spinning drum, and a machine older than some of our customers.

01

Send us a photo

Any photo. Even the blurry ones — especially the blurry ones. Grain forgives everything.

02

We split the colors

Your photo becomes two halftone layers, one per ink. This is where the magic math lives.

03

The drum spins

Each layer prints in a single pass. Alignment: approximate. On purpose. Mostly.

04

It ships flat

Board-backed, never rolled, with a note about which happy accidents happened to yours.

The Machine

Meet Rosie.
She is the studio.

One secondhand risograph duplicator, born 1998, bought for the price of a decent office chair. Everything on this page comes out of her.

1

Paper feed

Eats 300 gsm municipal card for breakfast. Jams on Mondays, like everyone.

2

The drum

One color per pass. Swapping inks takes eleven seconds — current studio record.

3

Control panel

Fourteen buttons from 1998. We trust four of them.

4

Output tray

Up to 150 prints a minute, when she's in the mood. She is rarely in the mood.

The Grain

The smudge
is the point.

Risograph isn't trying to be perfect — that's what makes it feel alive. Up close, your photo dissolves into dots, drift, and glow. These are the features:

Misregistration

The two layers land about a millimetre apart, differently every pass. Twins, never clones.

Halftone dots

Zoom in and the image becomes rhythm — thousands of dots doing an impression of your photo.

The fluoro glow

Fluorescent pink reflects more light than it receives. Physics, but make it fun.
