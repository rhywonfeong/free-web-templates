ARCHIVE STELLAR REMNANTS GARGANTUA

The Gargantua Singularity

A supermassive rotating black hole located 10 billion light-years from Earth, characterized by a luminous accretion disk and severe gravitational time dilation.

FIG 1.A: SIMULATED HORIZON

01 Physical Properties

Gargantua's mass is estimated to be approximately 100 million solar masses. It spins at 99.8% of the speed of light, dragging the fabric of spacetime around it—a phenomenon known as frame-dragging. The accretion disk is hot enough to emit massive amounts of thermal radiation, yet the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) is dangerously close to the event horizon.

"The gravity is so strong that light itself is bent into a halo, creating a visual paradox where the back of the disk is visible above the black hole." — Dr. Amelia Brand

02 Temporal Mechanics

Due to the extreme gravitational potential, time dilation near Gargantua is severe. On the orbiting planet Miller, one hour equates to seven years on Earth. This creates significant tactical and logistical challenges for interstellar travelers attempting to retrieve data from the surface.
