Our Mission

Why AADJ Took on Medical Records

The problem in one sentence: Disability claimants are denied, not because they aren't disabled, but because they cannot access their own medical records.

Efficiency

Evidence delays are case delays. The number one biggest inefficiency at SSA is manually requesting medical records.

Chart Graphic

Guaranteed Access

The law prohibits information blocking and requires access, but real-world compliance is inconsistent.

Lock Iconography

The Incomplete Records Problem

Without full longitudinal records, key impairments are missed, RFCs are flawed, and meritorious claims are denied.

Right to Individual Access

Individual Access Services (IAS) empowers claimants — and their representatives — to obtain records directly, reducing dependency on slow, costly, and incomplete traditional record requests.

Understanding the Problem

What is
Information Blocking?

Healthcare systems create barriers — intentional or not — that prevent patients from accessing their own records.

Process Graphic

Fragmentation

Multiple portals with restrictions and partial records force patients to piece together their own history.

Incomplete Records

Missing treatment notes or imaging reports leave critical gaps that undermine disability claims.

Access Delays

Requiring written authorizations and manual requests adds weeks or months to the process.

Cost

Charging retrieval and per-page fees creates financial barriers that punish claimants for accessing what's legally theirs.

Technical Barriers

Inability to transmit usable data across systems makes electronic records practically inaccessible.

Selective Sharing

Requiring multiple logins and separate authorizations per provider makes comprehensive access nearly impossible.
