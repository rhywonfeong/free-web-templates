Nanovest – Portfolio Dashboard Redesign

Role: Lead UI/UX Designer · Platform: Mobile & Web · Duration: 3 months

Product Design UX Research Design System

Overview

Nanovest’s portfolio dashboard needed to scale for advanced users while staying approachable for new investors. I redesigned the experience to surface key insights, reduce cognitive load, and create a clear visual rhythm in dark mode.

Problem

Users struggled to understand total performance, asset distribution, and risk exposure at a glance. Information was scattered, and dark mode contrast made it to scan quickly.

Research & Insights

  • Interviewed active users to map daily decision-making patterns.
  • Analyzed session recordings to identify friction in navigation and comprehension.
  • Benchmarked leading fintech dashboards to inform hierarchy and chart usage.

Design Approach

I reorganized the dashboard into clear layers: high-level portfolio health, asset breakdown, and actionable insights. I defined a token-based dark mode system to ensure consistent contrast and elevation.

  • Created new information architecture and navigation pattern.
  • Designed modular cards for assets, performance, and insights.
  • Aligned visuals with an updated design system for Nanovest.

Final Design & Impact

The new dashboard helped users understand portfolio health in under 5 seconds and reduced navigation loops between screens.

  • Improved task success rate for “check portfolio performance” in usability tests.
  • Higher perceived clarity and trust from user feedback sessions.

What I Learned

Fintech design requires balancing precision with simplicity. Designing for dark mode at scale means treating contrast, motion, and hierarchy as part of the system— not just visual polish.

Savings Onboarding & Risk Profiling Flow

Role: UI/UX Designer · Platform: Mobile · Focus: Onboarding, flows, and decision support

User Flows Prototyping Testing

Overview

I designed a guided onboarding and risk profiling flow to help users start saving and investing with confidence. The flow balances regulatory requirements with a friendly, understandable experience.

Problem

Many users abandoned onboarding when faced with complex questions and jargon. Risk categories felt abstract and unrelatable, especially for first-time investors.

Research & Insights

  • Ran usability tests with prototypes to identify confusing steps.
  • Collaborated with product & legal to simplify language while staying compliant.
  • Mapped emotional states during onboarding to reduce drop-off moments.

Design Approach

I broke the flow into clear, short steps with progressive disclosure. Each risk level is explained visually and with everyday language, supported by microcopy and examples.

  • Introduced a stepper and clear progress indicators.
  • Redesigned question cards with better spacing and tap targets.
  • Created reusable components for future flows.

Final Design & Impact

With the new flow, users could complete onboarding faster with fewer errors, while understanding what their profile meant in practice.

  • Higher completion rate for onboarding in usability testing sessions.
  • Reduced support questions around risk levels and plan selection.

What I Learned

Complex flows can feel light when the right pacing, tone, and visuals are in place. Small details in copy and micro-interactions can significantly change how secure users feel.
