Liquidity provision remains one of the cornerstone activities in Decentralized Finance (DeFi). However, the concept of Impermanent Loss (IL) continues to be a barrier for many institutional and retail investors alike. As we move into 2024, the mechanics of Automated Market Makers (AMMs) have evolved, and so have the strategies to mitigate this risk.

# What is Impermanent Loss?

Impermanent loss happens when you provide liquidity to a liquidity pool, and the price of your deposited assets changes compared to when you deposited them. The bigger this change is, the more you are exposed to impermanent loss. In this case, the loss means less dollar value at the time of withdrawal than at the time of deposit.

Key Takeaway

IL isn't "real" until you withdraw your liquidity. If prices return to their original ratios, the loss disappears—hence the name "impermanent".

The standard formula for calculating divergence loss in a constant product market maker (x * y = k) is derived as follows:

calculation.ts 
// Calculate Impermanent Loss based on price ratio change
function calculateImpermanentLoss(priceRatio: number): number {
  const numerator = 2 * Math.sqrt(priceRatio);
  const denominator = 1 + priceRatio;
  
  return (numerator / denominator) - 1;
}

// Example: Price doubles (ratio = 2)
const loss = calculateImpermanentLoss(2.0); 
// Result: -0.057 or -5.7%

# Hedging Strategies

To combat this, sophisticated LPs employ delta-neutral strategies. By borrowing the volatile asset to provide liquidity, you can effectively short the asset while earning fees on it. If the asset price drops, your debt value decreases, offsetting the IL.

"The goal of a liquidity provider shouldn't just be yield farming; it should be preserving principal while capturing volatility fees."
