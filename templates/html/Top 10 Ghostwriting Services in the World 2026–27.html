What Makes a Ghostwriting Service Worth Considering?


Choosing a ghostwriter involves much more than looking at a company's rating. A professional manuscript can take months to develop, so authors should evaluate the entire working relationship before committing.


1. Relevant Writing Experience


Look for experience related to your specific genre. A ghostwriter experienced with business books may have a very different skill set from one who specializes in memoirs or fiction.


2. Author Voice


Voice is one of the most important elements of ghostwriting. The final book should represent the author's ideas and personality rather than sounding like generic content.


3. Research Quality


Research requirements vary widely. A technical, historical, business, or educational manuscript may require considerably more research than a short personal project.


4. Communication


Authors should understand how often they will receive updates and who will be responsible for project communication.


5. Revision Policy


Before signing a contract, determine how revisions work and whether additional rounds of changes involve extra costs.


6. Confidentiality


Ghostwriters may receive private information, unpublished ideas, business material, and personal stories. Confidentiality should be addressed clearly in the agreement.


7. Ownership


The contract should clearly explain manuscript ownership, intellectual-property rights, attribution, and any other relevant rights.


8. Portfolio


Always review relevant samples. A polished website alone does not demonstrate whether a company can produce the type of manuscript you need.
