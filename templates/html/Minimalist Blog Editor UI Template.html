Minimalism isn't just about using less; it's about making room for what matters. In the digital age, where attention is the scarcest currency, interface design has gravitated towards cleaner, flatter, and more functional aesthetics.

When we strip away the ornamental shadows and bevels of the skeuomorphic era, we are left with the raw components of communication: typography, spacing, and color.

"Perfection is achieved, not when there is nothing more to add, but when there is nothing left to take away." — Antoine de Saint-Exupéry

The Role of Typography

Typography plays a pivotal role in modern web design. With fewer graphical elements to guide the eye, the weight, size, and tracking of fonts become the primary tools for establishing hierarchy.

Design systems like Linear and Vercel have popularized a specific flavor of minimalism: subtle borders, translucent backdrops, and a strict adherence to grid systems. This isn't just aesthetic—it's highly functional, allowing complex data to be presented without overwhelming the user.
